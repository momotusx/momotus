# Design decision

## The v3 pick (2026-10-21)

The owner's words, translated: "I told you Apple TV: the colors and everything should be the same." So v3 is the direction closest to the Apple TV app, which both design critics named in every round: V2 Top Shelf (`design/exploration/v3/index.html`, five review rounds, 10 of 10 on TV and phone, `plans/reviews/design-v3-*`). Its colors follow Apple TV's own, as measured in `design/reference/apple-tv.md`: a black or near-black stage where the artwork is the only color, white text at Apple's opacity levels, 10 percent hairlines, a white focus (scale, lift, shadow, specular, never a colored ring), the white primary pill, and Apple's system blue only where Apple uses a key color (sheet primaries, sign-in, links). This replaces the blue-green accent switch of the exploration and the v2 accent #E5372A. If the owner wants another direction or a different key color, this block is where it changes.

Details decided with it, by the same rule (match Apple TV, except where the owner ruled a color out): switches use Apple's own green on iOS and Android (`#30D158` dark) as the iOS Settings switch does; destructive actions (Delete account, Remove) stay without red, as the owner ruled red out, and use the key blue in alerts with the destructive word itself; the TV form has no key color at all, as tvOS; provider sign-in buttons keep their provider's own colors (Facebook blue, Google's mark), since each provider draws its own button; the subtitle picker's language flags (HatScripts circle-flags, MIT, drawn as images, never emoji) keep theirs; and the title page's ratings row shows IMDb's, TMDB's and Trakt's own marks in their own colors (the owner, 2026-10-24: "like on the covers"; files and sources in `design/marks/`), the only other colors in the chrome. The built exploration is `design/exploration/v3/v2.html`; the draft language and tokens are `design/exploration/v3/drafts/`.

## Owner direction, later on 2026-10-21

The owner's words, translated: study every page of Apple's design in detail, on the phone and on the web, and make our phone, web and desktop designs exactly that way; IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes ratings sit side by side, nicely, by default, always; and take from Stremio's design too, which is not bad overall.

What this sets: phone layouts follow Apple's iPhone TV app (`design/reference/apple-tv.md`, iPhone sections); web and desktop layouts follow tv.apple.com as measured there (its navigation, hover and keyboard states, shelves and pages), not the TV canvas scaled; the TV form follows tvOS and is checked on Android TV; Stremio's good parts are studied in `design/reference/stremio.md` and borrowed where they fit this language; every title shows its IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes ratings side by side wherever a rating appears (hero, title page, cards where ratings show), as Apple's TV app shows Rotten Tomatoes, by default and without any setting.

Addon text and emoji (2026-10-21): addons send their own text with emoji (a floppy disk before a size, flags before languages). The no-emoji rule covers everything Marquee shows, so the core strips emoji and pictographs from third-party text it passes through and keeps the words; flags become the language name the core already knows. The core's own captions stay as written. The 12 borrow items in `design/reference/stremio.md` are adopted, each in the v3 language rather than in the reference's "In v3" sketch where the two differ (no glass in content, no hairline pills, no outlined buttons, white focus):

1. The stream panel beside the info: a solid `surface` panel with no edge; it holds the episodes, then a chosen episode's streams, in place (the title page's panel pattern), so there is no separate episodes or versions page.
2. Streams in addon order with the All or per-addon filter: the filter is a pill menu like the season picker, shown only when two or more addons answered; the core's pick and its plain reason sit on top.
3. The last-used source: a white progress line under its name.
4. The info column order: logo, one meta row with the ratings row, the chips, the summary, the action row.
5. Genres and directors as linked chips: filled pills (the 16 percent fill over art, `surface` on the page), no hairline. The cast shows once, in the Cast shelf (and the Starring line on desktop), never as chips too.
6. Library and Watched as two small actions in the action row: Add to Library, then Watched (a movie's alone), each its icon in the circle and its word under it; Watched's check fills its circle once marked. The paired capsule and the big Mark as watched pill beside Play are gone (the owner, 2026-10-23).
7. Episode panel tools: Prev and Next around the season menu, an episode search field, monochrome Watched and Upcoming chips.
8. Discover's select-then-open: a solid `surface` preview panel on wide screens.
9. The addon catalog and details sheet: rows on `surface`; a row's Install is the plain secondary off the TV (one primary per screen) and the white pill on the focused TV row, Remove the plain secondary (no outline); in the sheet, Install the key-fill primary off the TV.
10. Search that takes links: the hints as plain text at 64 percent (tertiary text fails 4.5:1).
11. Settings as one page with a section menu, on desktop: solid grouped lists, hairlines between rows only.
12. The subtitle menu, as plans/player-polish.md 2.4 adapts it: languages first with a search field and flags, then a level per language (a sheet on the phone, a stepped panel on desktop, two columns on TV); strong glass, since it floats over the picture, as the player's other menus do.

## The language

The Apple TV app's own look. A near-black stage where the artwork is the only color; white text at Apple's opacity levels; solid grey surfaces with 10 percent hairlines; fills instead of outlines; a white focus on TV that lifts the item and draws no ring; the white primary pill; Apple's system blue only where Apple uses a key color.
